# Ghanaian Languages & Speech Acoustics Guide 🇬🇭🗣️

## Supported Dialects & Phonology

Yenkasa provides native-level accommodation for six major Ghanaian linguistic varieties:

### 1. Asante Twi & Akuapem Twi (Akan)
- **Tonal Structure**: Akan is a tone language with two level tones: **High** and **Low**. Tone carries grammatical and lexical distinction (e.g. *pápá* = father, *pàpà* = good, *pàpâ* = fan).
- **Vowel Harmony**: Operates on Advanced Tongue Root (ATR) vowel harmony:
  - **+ATR**: /i/, /e/, /u/, /o/, /æ/
  - **-ATR**: /ɪ/, /ɛ/, /ʊ/, /ɔ/, /a/
- **Consonant Palatalization**:
  - `ky` /tɕ/ (similar to English *ch* in *chair*, but alveolo-palatal)
  - `gy` /dʑ/ (similar to English *j* in *jar*)
  - `tw` /tɕʷ/ (labialized alveolo-palatal affricate)
  - `hy` /ɕ/ (voiceless alveolo-palatal fricative)

### 2. Fante (Mfantse)
- Spoken across Central and Western coastal Ghana.
- Retains distinctive final consonants and vowel nasalizations (e.g. *edziban* for food, *ahoɔhia* for emergency).

### 3. Gã (Accra Plains)
- **Classification**: Kwa family, spoken extensively in Accra, Tema, Osu, and Jamestown.
- **Double Articulations / Labial-Velars**:
  - `kp` /k͡p/ (voiceless labial-velar stop)
  - `gb` /ɡ͡b/ (voiced labial-velar stop)
  - `ŋ` /ŋ/ (velar nasal)
- **Tone**: High, Mid, Low with complex tone terrace downdrift.

### 4. Ewe (Èʋegbe)
- **Classification**: Gbe group, spoken in Volta Region.
- **Unique Bilabial Fricatives**:
  - `ƒ` /ɸ/ (voiceless bilabial fricative)
  - `ʋ` /β/ (voiced bilabial fricative)
  - `ɖ` /ɖ/ (voiced retroflex stop)
- **Extreme Vowel Nasalization**: All cardinal vowels have contrastive nasal counterparts: ã, ẽ, ĩ, õ, ũ.

### 5. Dagbani (Dagbanli)
- **Classification**: Gur family (Mole-Dagbani group), spoken in Northern Ghana (Tamale, Yendi).
- **Vocalic Nuances**:
  - Characteristic vowel elongation and velar/glottal transitions (e.g. *Desiba*, *Suhudoo*, *Alaafee*).
